# Synthetic Interview Notes

> SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA. These are simulated notes, not interviews with real participants.

## Method

Five fictional task walkthroughs were authored to demonstrate qualitative synthesis. Labels P1–P5 are synthetic participant identifiers.

## Notes

- P1, an adult booking for themself, said they usually book during a short work break. They wanted to see the earliest available appointment without opening each clinic.
- P2, a caregiver, was unsure whether the form should contain their contact information or the patient's. They wanted confirmation sent to both people but raised consent concerns.
- P3 relied on keyboard navigation in the simulated walkthrough. They could not infer slot availability when the only described difference was color.
- P4 abandoned a mock form after entering several fields because the session-warning behavior was unclear. They worried that sensitive information might disappear.
- P5 selected a slot that became unavailable before submission. They expected alternatives near the same time rather than restarting clinic selection.

## Repeated patterns

- P1, P4, and P5 emphasized completing the task quickly.
- P2 and P4 were uncertain about data handling and consequences.
- P3 and P5 needed status information that did not rely on color alone.

## Contradictions and limitations

- P1 preferred SMS; P4 preferred email and did not want SMS.
- P2's caregiver scenario is not evidence of legal consent requirements.
- Five authored walkthroughs cannot establish prevalence.
- No evidence covers screen-reader behavior, low vision, older adults as a group, or languages other than English.
